Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of artificial intelligence that helps computers understand, interpret, and manipulate human language.
The primary objective of Natural Language Processing is to enable machines to read text, hear speech, and extract valuable meaning from unstructured data.
Many modern technologies depend on NLP techniques to provide intelligent user experiences.

Tokenization is the foundational process of breaking text into smaller units called tokens, such as words or sentences.
Without tokenization, machines cannot easily analyze linguistic structures.

Stop-word removal is a text cleaning technique where common words such as 'is', 'the', and 'at' are filtered out because they carry little contextual significance.
Removing stop words helps focus computational attention on informative keywords.

Part-of-Speech tagging is an NLP method that labels each word in a sentence with its corresponding grammatical category, such as nouns, verbs, or adjectives.
POS tagging enables syntactic analysis and rule-based information extraction.

Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) is a statistical measure used to evaluate the importance of a word in a document collection.
TF-IDF gives higher weight to terms that appear frequently in a specific sentence but infrequently across the entire corpus.
TF-IDF enables computers to identify dominant domain concepts automatically.

Question generation refers to the automated task of creating relevant questions from informative source text.
Rule-based question generation utilizes grammatical rules and syntactic patterns to transform declarative statements into interrogative questions.
FAQ generation systems save significant time by automatically summarizing documents and providing direct answers for users.
